How to Choose a VPS in India (2026)

By Gen X Web Hosting
•Nov 12, 2026•16 min read

Quick Answer (Key Takeaways)

To choose a VPS in India, size vCPU and RAM to the workload, pick enough SSD or NVMe storage and bandwidth, and place the server close to your users. Prefer KVM virtualization with full root or Administrator access. Pay in INR, add your GSTIN, and confirm the data-centre city before you buy.

A virtual private server sits between shared hosting and a dedicated machine. You get a set amount of CPU, RAM and disk, and you can usually log in as root on Linux or Administrator on Windows. That is the right move when a site or app has outgrown shared hosting, or when you need software a shared panel will not install.

When a VPS is the right move

Stay on shared hosting if you are launching a straightforward site, you are happy in a control panel, and traffic is still modest.

Choose a VPS when you need root or Administrator access, you want fixed CPU and RAM, or the site, shop, or database has hit shared-hosting limits.

If you are still deciding whether you need hosting at all, start with domain versus web hosting. If you already need your own server, see VPS hosting for Indian businesses.

Size the specs to the workload

Four numbers matter: RAM, vCPU, storage, and bandwidth, including the network-speed cap. RAM usually runs out first, because the OS, the app, the database, and any workers all share it. vCPU matters once work runs in parallel. Storage must cover the OS, the app, the database, logs, and backups — and you should know whether the disk is NVMe. Bandwidth is the monthly transfer allowance; network speed (100 Mbps versus 300 Mbps, for example) is how fast that data can move.

Treat the table as a starting point, not a guarantee. You can often move up later. Gen X advertises provision-free upgrades on its KVM cloud plans.

Managed vs unmanaged

Unmanaged (or self-managed) means you get the virtual machine and, usually, full root or Administrator access. You handle guest updates, the firewall, and the application. The provider looks after the host hardware, the hypervisor, and the network.

Managed means the provider also takes on some or all of the software. Ask what they patch, what they monitor, and what is still your job.

On Gen X Linux Virtual Cloud plans you get full root SSH access and you install your own software. The page also lists 24/7 host-node monitoring, DDoS protection, automated daily backups, and optional cPanel/WHM or DirectAdmin. That is host-level care, not application management.

If you do not want to maintain the server, use a managed product for that app, such as managed n8n or a one-click template on Nivi Apps.

KVM, NVMe and bandwidth

KVM is a common, hardware-backed way to split a physical server into isolated instances, each with its own allocated CPU and RAM. When a provider says “dedicated resources,” ask whether those figures are reserved for you. Gen X’s Linux and Windows cloud pages both describe KVM with dedicated CPU and RAM.

NVMe SSD is the sensible default in 2026 for web apps and databases. Gen X’s Linux Virtual Cloud page describes high-speed NVMe SSD storage; the plan cards list the size as “SSD Storage.” If disk speed matters, confirm the type on the plan you are buying.

Check both monthly transfer and the Mbps cap. On Gen X Linux plans, published caps run from 100 Mbps on NODE 1X to 300 Mbps on NODE 4X–6X, with transfer from 1 TB to 12 TB. Windows plans follow the same pattern from BASIC (100 Mbps, 1 TB) to PLATINUM (300 Mbps, 8 TB). For large backups or media, the cap can matter as much as the allowance.

Backups

A VPS is not backed up just because it exists. Ask whether backups are automatic, where they are stored, how long they are kept, and how you restore.

Gen X’s Linux Virtual Cloud page lists automated daily backups. The Windows Virtual Cloud page lists daily backups of the Windows environment and MSSQL databases. Neither page, in the text we reviewed, publishes a retention period, so ask support how restores work. Keep your own off-server copy of anything you cannot afford to rebuild.

Operating system and control panel

Choose the OS from the software you need.

  • Linux is the usual choice for PHP, WordPress, most Node and Python apps, and tools such as n8n. You manage it over SSH. Gen X Linux VPS plans offer an optional cPanel/WHM or DirectAdmin licence; those prices are not listed on the plan page, so check at checkout. See cPanel vs DirectAdmin.
  • Windows is the usual choice for ASP.NET, .NET, IIS, and Microsoft SQL Server. You connect with full Administrator RDP. Gen X Windows Virtual Cloud plans support ASP.NET Core, .NET 8, IIS 10 and MSSQL Server 2022, and list Plesk (Plesk Obsidian in the feature copy) as available.

The same split is described on Linux vs Windows hosting, which is written around shared plans. Linux plan cards do not name a default distribution; the OpenClaw VPS page mentions Ubuntu 24.04 for that product. If you need a specific image, ask before you order.

Location, latency, GST and payments

For users in India, the server city affects how quickly pages and APIs respond. A VPS in an Indian metro will usually feel snappier for Indian visitors than one in Europe or the US. If your customers or APIs are mostly overseas, pick a region close to them, not automatically an Indian one.

Do not assume an Indian company means an Indian data centre. Gen X Web Hosting is based in Lucknow, has been hosting since 2011, and is part of Nivi Cloud Pvt. Ltd. Its privacy policy says servers sit in multiple data centres owned and operated by the company or its partners, and does not name cities. The Linux and Windows VPS plan pages also do not name a city. Managed n8n hosting does list Delhi, Mumbai and Pune among its locations, alongside several overseas regions. Before you buy any VPS, ask which city the instance will be in.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and the DPDP Rules, 2025, do not, as a general rule, require every Indian business to keep all personal data in India. Cross-border transfers are generally allowed, though the government can restrict particular countries, and tighter sector rules — such as Reserve Bank of India requirements on payment-system data — can still apply. An Indian data centre can still be right for latency, policy, or a sector rule, but it is not automatic DPDP compliance. This is not legal advice. If you handle regulated data, take advice and ask the host in writing where the server and backups sit.

Look for prices in INR, a GST invoice, and a payment method you can use. As of 12 November 2026, Linux Virtual Cloud monthly prices start at ₹799 (NODE 1X) and Windows Virtual Cloud at ₹919 (BASIC). The plan pages do not say whether those figures include GST, so confirm at checkout. Add a GSTIN in the client area and it appears on later invoices. Payment methods in the knowledge base are an online payment gateway or bank transfer. If you need UPI, confirm it in the cart or with billing.

A short checklist before you buy

  • RAM and vCPU are a comfortable starting point for the workload, not the bare minimum.
  • Disk will hold the app, database and backups, and you know whether it is NVMe.
  • Monthly transfer and the Mbps cap will cover busy days and backup jobs.
  • Virtualization is KVM (or another model you accept), with resources reserved for you.
  • You know who applies updates, and whether a control panel licence is included or extra.
  • You have a confirmed data-centre city, close enough to your users or APIs.
  • Backups are on, you know how to restore, and you also have your own copy.
  • The quote is in INR, you know how GST will appear, and you can pay by a method you already use.
  • Uptime and refund terms are in writing. Gen X quotes 99.9% uptime and lists phone support on +91 9429692542, 10:00 am–7:30 pm IST, Monday–Saturday, with live chat, email and tickets 24/7.

Frequently asked questions

When should I move from shared hosting to a VPS?
When you need root or Administrator access, you want fixed CPU and RAM, or the site or database has outgrown the shared plan. If you are happy in a control panel and traffic is still modest, shared hosting is simpler.

How much RAM do I need for a VPS in India?
Start from the workload, not the country. A quiet site can begin at 2 GB, a typical WordPress site is more comfortable at 4 GB, and shops or self-hosted automation often need 8 GB or more. Watch memory use after you go live.

Does a VPS for Indian users have to be in an Indian data centre?
No. An Indian city usually means better latency for users in India, and some sector rules can require local storage, but the DPDP Act does not generally force every business to keep all personal data in India. Confirm the server city with the provider. Gen X’s VPS plan pages do not name a city; its managed n8n page lists Delhi, Mumbai and Pune among other locations.

What is the difference between managed and unmanaged VPS?
Unmanaged means you administer the guest server yourself. Managed means the provider takes on some or all of that work. Host monitoring and backups can be included without the provider managing your applications.
