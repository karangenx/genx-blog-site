<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you run an agency, a freelance practice or a small hosting brand in India, a Linux reseller plan is usually the step before you rent a whole server. <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/start-hosting-reseller-business-india.php">Gen X Web Hosting</a> has been hosting since 2011, is based in Lucknow, and lists a <strong>99.9%</strong> uptime SLA on these plans.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Linux reseller hosting is</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Reseller hosting is a middle layer. You buy a block of resources — disk, bandwidth and a set number of control-panel accounts — then you split that block into packages and sell those packages to your clients. You invoice them and set the limits. The data centre, the panel licence, the backups listed on the plan, and the support behind the server stay with the provider.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is different from shared hosting, where you get one cPanel for your own site. It is also different from a <a href="https://blog.genxwhosting.com/blog/how-to-choose-a-vps-in-india-2026/">VPS</a>, where you get root access and you are responsible for the operating system.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A Linux reseller plan is the usual choice when most of your clients run PHP apps, especially WordPress. If a client needs ASP.NET or Microsoft SQL Server, that work belongs on a <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/hosting.php?type=windows-reseller">Windows Plesk reseller plan</a> instead — more on that below.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">WHM vs cPanel: who uses which</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On a cPanel reseller plan you get two panels, not one.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>WHM</strong> is your admin desk. From WHM you create packages, open and suspend accounts, work with DNS zones, and change a client&apos;s resource limits. You use this tool; your clients do not log into it.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>cPanel</strong> is what each client sees. In cPanel they upload files, create mailboxes, issue SSL, and install apps. If you already hand clients a cPanel login today, this is the familiar screen.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">cPanel is the end-user panel; WHM is the reseller panel that sits above it. Gen X also has a <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/hosting.php?type=linux-reseller">Linux reseller plan on DirectAdmin</a> if your team already works in that panel. The idea is the same — a reseller area plus a client area — but the screens and menu names differ.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For the click-by-click path, the knowledgebase has <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/knowledgebase/42/How-to-create-package-in-whm.html">how to create a package in WHM</a> and a <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/knowledgebase/93/Getting-Started-with-Linux-reseller-hosting.html">Linux reseller getting-started guide</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How to build packages</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A package is a named bundle of limits: disk, bandwidth, number of websites or addon domains, email accounts, databases and FTP accounts. You create two or three packages that match how you actually sell — for example a starter site, a business site and a shop — then you assign a package when you create the account.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Keep the math honest. Every client&apos;s disk and bandwidth come out of your reseller plan, not out of thin air. If you sell &ldquo;unlimited&rdquo; disk to ten clients on a plan with a fixed web-space figure, you will run out. A safer habit is to set a real disk number on each package, leave a little headroom on the plan, and upgrade the reseller plan when the roster grows.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Searches for &ldquo;unlimited Linux reseller hosting&rdquo; are common. Read the plan card, not the headline. On the current Gen X cPanel/WHM cards, &ldquo;unlimited&rdquo; applies to some items (email on every plan; websites on Ultimate) while account counts and disk stay capped.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Private nameservers and white-label: what clients see</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">White-label, on these plans, means your clients should see your brand, not the upstream host&apos;s. The plan page lists custom private nameservers such as <code>ns1.yourdomain.com</code> and <code>ns2.yourdomain.com</code>, so the DNS experience stays yours.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In practice you will:</p>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
  <li>Register or point two hostnames (<code>ns1</code> and <code>ns2</code>) for your brand.</li>
  <li>Set those as the nameservers for each client domain, instead of the provider&apos;s default names.</li>
  <li>Keep your own invoices, support mailbox and website under that brand.</li>
</ol>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Clients still get a cPanel login. They should not need to know who runs the servers. If you also sell the domain, this is the same nameserver conversation you have when a domain and a hosting account first come together — see <a href="https://blog.genxwhosting.com/blog/domain-vs-web-hosting-what-indian-businesses-actually-need/">domain vs web hosting</a> for the basics.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why CloudLinux comes up in reseller searches</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&ldquo;Reseller hosting with CloudLinux&rdquo; shows up because noisy neighbours are the classic shared-server problem. One client&apos;s runaway script, a traffic spike or a compromised site can steal CPU and memory from everyone else on the box.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">CloudLinux is software that sits on the server and isolates accounts. On the Gen X cPanel/WHM reseller page it is listed as <strong>CloudLinux &amp; CageFS isolation</strong>: each cPanel account runs in its own virtualized environment. The DirectAdmin reseller page describes the same idea as a Lightweight Virtual Environment (LVE) for each account, so a busy neighbour is less able to pull your other clients down.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">CloudLinux does not turn a reseller plan into a VPS. You still share the machine. It does put a clearer resource fence between client accounts, which is why agencies ask for it before they put paying sites on a reseller server.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Who should choose what</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>A freelancer with a handful of WordPress clients.</strong> A Starter or Basic cPanel/WHM (or DirectAdmin) reseller plan is usually enough. You get a proper panel for each client, white-label nameservers, and room to add a few more sites without jumping to a VPS.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>An agency that already bills monthly for care and hosting.</strong> Size the plan by account count and disk, not by a marketing &ldquo;unlimited&rdquo; line. If most work is WordPress, stay on Linux. Keep a Windows reseller plan only if you regularly take .NET or MSSQL work.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Someone who wants to start a hosting brand from scratch.</strong> Read the <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/start-hosting-reseller-business-india.php">reseller business guide</a> first. The plan gives you the panel and the server; you still need nameservers, packages, terms, billing and support hours your clients can rely on.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>A shop that only needs one or two of your own sites.</strong> You do not need a reseller plan. A <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/hosting.php?type=linux-sharedcp">Linux shared cPanel</a> account is the simpler fit.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">When to move from reseller to a VPS or a dedicated server</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Move up when at least one of these is true:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li>You are bumping the account, website or disk ceiling on your current plan, and a larger reseller tier is no longer comfortable.</li>
  <li>You need root access, a custom stack, or workloads a reseller panel will not let you install.</li>
  <li>One client needs an isolated server of their own, not a package on your shared reseller pool.</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/nodex.php?type=linux-virtual-cloud">Linux Virtual Cloud (Node X) VPS</a> lists full root SSH, KVM resources, optional cPanel/WHM or DirectAdmin licences, and automated daily backups. If even a VPS is too tight, look at <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/dedicated-server.php">dedicated servers</a>. See also <a href="https://blog.genxwhosting.com/blog/shared-vs-vps-hosting/">shared vs VPS hosting</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">FAQ</h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">How many accounts can I host on a Linux reseller plan?</h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On the current Gen X cPanel/WHM cards, Starter lists 10 cPanel accounts, Basic 25, Premium 100 and Ultimate 200 (Ultimate also lists unlimited websites). Confirm the live plan card before you buy.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Do I need WHMCS or another billing panel?</h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The reseller plan pages do not list a billing panel as part of the plan. You invoice your clients yourself. Some resellers add a separate billing tool; that is optional and sits outside the hosting plan.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What do my clients actually see?</h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They get a cPanel login (or a DirectAdmin client login, on that line). With private nameservers and branding set up, they should see your brand on DNS and in your own invoices and support, not the upstream host&apos;s.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Is CloudLinux the same as a VPS?</h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">No. CloudLinux isolates accounts on a shared server. A VPS gives you a virtual machine with your own resources and, on the Gen X Linux VPS page, full root SSH.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Can I move existing client accounts to a new reseller plan?</h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The cPanel/WHM reseller page lists free migration of existing cPanel reseller accounts. Ask support to confirm the process for your roster. The plan pages also list a 30-day money-back guarantee; the refund conditions are in the <a href="https://genxwhosting.com/pages/terms.php">Terms of Use</a>.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">When should I leave reseller hosting for a VPS?</h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When you outgrow the plan limits, need root or a custom stack, or have a client who needs a server of their own. Start with the VPS guide linked above.</p>
